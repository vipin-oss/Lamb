# Task 2 redo, second pass — the fourteen-row matrix with l_g > 0

Setup of record: half-thickness `h = 5e-4` m, elastic isotropic constants of aluminium in whichever
paper is being tested, every coupling off, nonlocal length 0, both lengths in the ratio `l_g/h`,
seven rows per face (fourteen in all), rows and columns equilibrated to maximum one in four passes,
smallest singular value on a 5 m/s grid with a parabola through the three lowest points. Code:
`internal/code/v8_scan.py` (`build_B`, `sigma_min`) and `internal/code/v8_run.py` (`branch`).

All three agree. The twenty-row level of the earlier round came from three things, in this order:
scanning the determinant instead of the singular value, a 100 m/s grid against a dip a few m/s
wide, and `h = 1e-3` with the ZnO constants and the couplings on.

One further fault was found and fixed in passing: `core.roots_eta` trimmed the polynomial at a
relative tolerance of `1e-25`, and in the nearly even polynomial of the elastic case the leading
coefficient is `4e-31` relative, so two roots were silently dropped and the fourteen-row build
returned twelve roots. The tolerance is now `1e-40`, above the `1e-51` rounding floor at sixty
digits. The A0 values above are unchanged by this fix and the S0 branch at `fd = 10` becomes
buildable, at the cost noted below.

Reading: the gradient length stiffens both branches, monotonically in `l/h`, by 0.2 to 5.7 per cent
at `l/h = 0.2`. The stiffening is larger on A0 than on S0 at the same length, and larger at the
lower frequency, which is what a length competing with the wavelength should do.

The `fd = 10` S0 row is not available and the reason is physical, not numerical. There
`k l_g` is `3e-4`, so the four partial waves generated by the gradient length collapse onto
`eta = +-1/l_g` to within `1e-7` of their own value: 40000.003 against 40000.007. Their columns
then differ in the eighth digit, the equilibrated singular value rounds to zero at every speed,
and the dip test has nothing to test. At `fd = 100` and 1000 the same four roots are separated
enough to resolve. This is the limit in which the size effect is out of range, and it should be
said so rather than reported as a zero.

## The thin-plate piezoelectric limit (first of the single-coupling limits)

Sumit paper, both lengths zero, carrier and heat terms switched off so that only the piezoelectric
coupling is live, faces electrically open (`D_z = 0`). S0 at `fd = 100`:

* dip at 5368 m/s (sigma_min 7.4e-4 at 5360 and 4.0e-3 at 5380, so the minimum is near 5368);
* the plane-stress open-circuit speed from the constants is 5509.6 m/s, the short-circuit speed
  5259.1 m/s.

So the model sits between the two, and the stiffening it produces is `rho (v^2 - v_short^2)`
= 6.6 GPa against 15.3 GPa from the plane-stress reduction, i.e. about forty per cent of the
closed-form value. That is not a rounding difference and it needs an explanation before it is
quoted: the candidate is that the faces are open in `D_z` only, so the in-plane field is not
short-circuited the way the plane-stress reduction assumes, and part of the piezoelectric
stiffening is missing. Recorded here as the first single-coupling number, to be settled with the
reviewer rather than written into a master.

## The closure (Task 3) — status unchanged, with the blocker identified

With both lengths equal and the couplings off, the fourteen-row build at `fd = 1000` gives
equilibrated singular values of order `1e-18` and below at every speed, for the variational rows
and for the constitutive rows alike, so the dip test there is meaningless and the earlier pair
(3486 against 3199.6) remains unconfirmed. The same collapse happens as at `fd = 10` above:
`l_nl > 0` in the elastic configuration produces near-duplicate columns. The closure has to be
compared in the fully coupled model, and that is the next piece of work.
