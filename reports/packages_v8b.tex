\section*{The correction of record}

The finding that the fourteen-row matrix is singular at every speed was wrong, and it came from the
test, not from the model. Three faults, in this order: the scan followed the determinant instead of
the smallest singular value, the grid was $100$~m/s wide against a dip a few m/s wide, and the
earlier runs sat at $l/h=2\times10^{-5}$ where four roots decay over a fraction of the plate. At
$h=5\times10^{-4}$~m with $l/h$ between $0.05$ and $0.2$ the equilibrated matrix has full rank and
the dips are clean.

A fourth fault was found while reproducing the case. The root finder trimmed the polynomial at a
relative tolerance of $10^{-25}$, and in the elastic case the leading coefficient sits at
$4\times10^{-31}$ of the largest one, so two roots were dropped and the fourteen-row build returned
twelve roots. The tolerance is now $10^{-40}$, above the rounding floor of $10^{-51}$ at sixty
digits. The A0 numbers below are unchanged by that fix.

The gradient length stiffens both branches, monotonically in $l/h$, by $0.2$ to $5.7$ per cent at
$l/h=0.2$. The stiffening is stronger on A0 than on S0 at the same length, and stronger at the lower

The missing S0 entries at $fd=10$ are physical, not numerical. There $k l_g$ is $3\times10^{-4}$, so

That is also the region where the size effect is out of range, and the masters should not quote it.

\section*{The first single-coupling limit: piezoelectric, thin plate}

Sumit paper, both lengths zero, carrier and thermal terms switched off so that only the
piezoelectric coupling is live, faces electrically open, $fd=100$. The S0 dip sits at $5368$~m/s.
The plane-stress open-circuit speed from the same constants is $5509.6$~m/s and the short-circuit
speed is $5259.1$~m/s, so the model lies between the two, and the stiffening it produces is
$6.6$~GPa against $15.3$~GPa from the closed-form reduction, about forty per cent.

That gap is too large to be rounding and it is not being written into a master until it is
explained. The likely reason is that our faces are open in $D_z$ only, so the in-plane field is not
held the way the plane-stress reduction assumes and part of the stiffening is missing. This is the
one point where a word from you would save a week.

\section*{The closure of the nonlocal boundary problem}

The earlier note said that the constitutive boundary conditions have nothing to act on. That is
withdrawn: the differential law carries homogeneous solutions $t\propto\exp(\pm z/l_{nl})$, the
partial-wave basis does not contain them because the nonlocal operator sits on the inertial term,
and omitting them amounts to setting their amplitudes to zero at both faces. The gradient length
does bring four partial waves, closed by the two double tractions per face; the nonlocal length
brings none, and nothing in the seven rows closes it.

The comparison itself is still not run. With both lengths equal and the couplings off, the
fourteen-row build gives equilibrated singular values of order $10^{-18}$ at every speed for both
closures, the same collapse as at $fd=10$ above, so the earlier pair ($3486$ against $3199.6$~m/s)
stays unconfirmed. The closure has to be compared in the fully coupled model, and that is the next
piece of work.
