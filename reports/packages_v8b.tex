\section*{The correction of record}

The finding that the fourteen-row matrix is singular at every speed was wrong, and it came from the
test, not from the model. Those runs put the two lengths at $10^{-8}$~m with a half-thickness of
$10^{-3}$~m, so the ratio $l/h$ was $2\times10^{-5}$. In that regime four of the fourteen roots
decay over a distance far shorter than the plate, their columns reach one face and not the other,
and a normalised determinant loses its level. With $l/h$ between $0.05$ and $0.2$ the same code
gives a matrix of full rank, and the smallest pivots are $2.7\times10^{-12}$, $4.8\times10^{-12}$,
$2.2\times10^{-11}$. The fourteen roots are not the trouble.

\section*{The assembled matrix reproduces the Rayleigh--Lamb speeds}

The check that matters is this one. Take the full ten-row assembly, all five fields coupled, turn
every coupling off, set both lengths to zero, put the isotropic constants of aluminium in whichever
paper you are testing, and scan the phase speed. The zeros of the determinant should sit on the
Rayleigh--Lamb branches of the same plate, which are known independently from the two-family solver
of the earlier round.

Every figure agrees with the reference solver to all four digits, and the tolerance asked for is
$10^{-5}$. This is a second and independent route to the same elastic limit, because the ten rows
here are the boundary conditions of the model, not the four-row block that the reference solver
uses. Neha and Anu show one extra dip at 6197.8~m/s. That is the bulk longitudinal speed of
aluminium: at $k=\omega/c_L$ one root of the matrix passes through $\eta=0$ and the determinant has
a degenerate zero there. It is not a plate mode and it is not counted.

Two things follow. The assembly, the row functions, the column scaling and the complex root finder
agree with the elastic benchmark through the boundary conditions themselves. And the singular-value
route and the determinant route now agree, which is item (e) of the task.

\section*{What is still not working, and what it is not}

With $l_g>0$ the same two routes still do not give a dip that can be trusted. At $l/h=0.05$, $0.1$,
$0.2$, all five fields coupled, working precision sixty digits and four passes of row and column
equilibration, the equilibrated determinant lies between $10^{-84}$ and $10^{-91}$ and wanders with
the speed without a reproducible minimum. A search of $3100$ to $3450$~m/s in steps of $10$ finds
nothing below the local level. Setting the four strongly evanescent columns to zero on the far face,
so that each is attached to the face it decays from, changes the level but not the wandering.

This is not precision and it is not the determinant. The ten-row case of the identical code is
exact. It is also not a difference of matrices: the assembly here and an independently written one
in the earlier round agree entry for entry, with a largest difference of zero, on a matrix whose
equilibrated smallest singular value comes out at $2.2\times10^{-16}$.

\section*{The closure of the nonlocal boundary problem}

The earlier note said that the constitutive boundary conditions of the literature have nothing to
act on in these models. That is withdrawn. The differential law carries homogeneous solutions
$t\propto\exp(\pm z/l_{nl})$, and the partial-wave basis $\exp(\eta_m z)$ does not contain them,
because the nonlocal operator sits on the inertial term and never becomes a factor of the
determinant. Omitting those solutions is the same as setting their amplitudes to zero at both
faces, and that is a choice which the seven rows do not justify. The gradient length does bring four
partial waves and the two double tractions per face do close them. The nonlocal length brings none,
and nothing in our set closes it.

The constitutive closure was written into the code for the two normal strains of the plate, in the
form $\mathrm{d}\varepsilon/\mathrm{d}z=\pm\varepsilon/l_{nl}$ at the two faces. One minimum appears
at $3486$~m/s at $l/h=0.1$ against $3199.6$~m/s with the lengths at zero, but against the behaviour
of the variational rows it cannot yet be called a mode. The masters should say that the seven rows
are a modelling choice of ours and that the equivalence with the integral law is open. The proposed
paragraph is in the internal note.
