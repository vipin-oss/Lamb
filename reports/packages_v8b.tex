\section*{The units fault}

You were right. The three tabulated heat capacities, $2.8\times10^{6}$, $2.6\times10^{6}$ and
$2.5\times10^{6}$, are volumetric already, and the code was multiplying by $\rho$ again. The heat
capacity was five thousand six hundred times too large in Sumit, and the corresponding factors in
Neha and Anu. `rcp_of` now returns the tabulated value. Every elastic and size-effect number
reported so far was produced with the couplings off and does not move; the fully coupled runs move
a little, and they now have their own baseline, $2181.9$~m/s at $fd=1000$ against the elastic
$2326.6$.

\section*{Hutson--White, bulk}

Sub-block $(u_z,\phi,\delta n)$ at $\eta=0$, only $e_{15}$ and the carriers, $D_n=0$, no
recombination, $f=100$~kHz. The peak of $|k_i/k_r|$ falls at $n_0=3\times10^{16}$~m$^{-3}$ against
$n_0^{*}=\varepsilon_{11}\omega/(q b_n)=2.965\times10^{16}$, one grid step, with
$|k_i/k_r|=1.86\times10^{-2}$ there. So the peak position is confirmed.

The sign is not. In this code's convention the root has $k_i<0$, that is growth, and four different
starting guesses converge to it, so it is not a root-selection artefact. Without drift a
piezosemiconductor should attenuate. Either the acoustoelectric term carries the opposite sign in
the v8 rows from the one the masters intend, or the model does amplify at $n_0^{*}$ and should say
so. I have not written either into a master.

The answer is independent of $D_n$ to the last digit, so the gap is not diffusion or Debye screening
inside the code, and at $D_n=0$ the carrier block loses its roots so that control cannot be
reported. The crossover is set by the carrier relaxation $-i\omega+1/\tau_R$ against $\omega$, and
it sits 30 to 300 times above the bulk $n_0^{*}$ because the plate test measures the static
stiffening at $kh\to0$, not the dielectric resonance that produces the bulk peak. That is what the
numbers say; the closed-form plate threshold is the next thing to write down, and I would rather do
that than assert a mechanism now.

\section*{The closure, with its own baseline}

$l_g=l_{nl}$ was the fault, as you said; with $0.5$ and $2\,l_g$ the runs are healthy. The baseline
column is the same coupled model with both lengths at zero, $2181.9$~m/s at $fd=1000$ and $943.5$ at
$fd=100$. The $l=10^{-8}$~m route to that baseline is not usable: at $l/h=2\times10^{-5}$ the
equilibrated singular value is $10^{-17}$ everywhere and turns negative in the fourth digit.

Against the baseline the sign follows the ratio: with $l_{nl}=0.5\,l_g$ all three entries stiffen
($+0.5$, $+1.8$, $+5.4$ per cent), with $l_{nl}=2\,l_g$ the first two soften ($-0.3$, $-1.3$ per
cent) and the third falls to $-16$ per cent. The alternative closure moves a given branch by at
most 26~m/s, 1.1 per cent, with no systematic sign.

The $0.2$, $0.5$ alternative entry was re-run over 800 to 2600~m/s in steps of 25: five shallow
minima, the deepest $8.3\times10^{-4}$ against a median of $2.6\times10^{-2}$, no clean branch, where
our rows give a single dip at 2300.7 with depth 87. It stays held.
